# US 290 toll, Austin to Manor

Asked four times on October 19, 2026, between 12:41 and 12:48: the travel-time
difference with no traffic, what that works out to as an hourly rate, the same
with traffic, and whether getting on or off partway changes the numbers.

## The road

- 290 Toll, the Manor Expressway: US 183 to east of SH 130, 6.2 miles, opened
  May 2014, run by the Central Texas Regional Mobility Authority.
- Three tolled mainlanes each way at 70 mph, grade separated, no signals. Three
  free frontage lanes each way at 55 mph west of Johnny Morris and 60 east of
  it, with 7 traffic signals.
- Everything either side of those 6.2 miles is the same road, so the whole
  question is mainlanes against frontage roads. The ShadowGlen entrance is off
  290 east of SH 130, where the tolled lanes end, so the full segment sits on
  the drive home rather than part of it.

## What it costs (2026 rates, 2-axle)

Each tolling point charges on its own and a trip pays the sum of the ones it
crosses. There is no distance proration and no surge: the rate is the same at
any hour.

| Tolling point, west to east | TxTag | By mail |
|---|---|---|
| US 183 ramps (direct connectors) | $0.75 | $1.13 |
| Springdale Rd ramps | $0.75 | $1.13 |
| Giles Ln ramps | $0.75 | $1.13 |
| Giles Ln mainline plaza | $1.53 | $2.30 |
| Harris Branch Pkwy ramps | $0.75 | $1.13 |
| Parmer Ln mainline plaza | $0.75 | $1.13 |
| 290/130 flyover | $0.77 | $1.16 |

A full run of the mainlanes passes both mainline plazas: **$2.28 with a TxTag**,
$3.43 by mail. The $1.53 quoted in the first three answers was the Giles plaza
alone; the rates below are the corrected ones.

## What it buys

- No traffic: about 4 minutes, and nearly all of it is the signals rather than
  the speed. Running time is 5.3 minutes tolled against 6.5 on the frontage
  roads; the rest is stops. Every light green and it saves about 1 minute, most
  of them red and it saves 7 to 8.
- Rush hour: 10 to 15 minutes, reported on this corridor rather than measured.
  The mainlanes hold near free-flow while each frontage signal goes from
  clearing a car or two to clearing a standing queue.
- So the toll costs about **$34 an hour** of time saved off-peak and about
  **$11 an hour** at peak (12 minutes saved). Because the rate never moves, the
  toll is an easier yes at rush hour than at midday - the opposite of how the
  variable-price MoPac express lanes behave.
- Eastbound in the evening, the tolled lanes end at SH 130 past Parmer and merge
  into the Manor signals, a stretch on the TxDOT top-100 congested list at about
  17 minutes of delay. Both routes share it, so it is bought upstream of that
  queue, not through it.

## Getting on or off partway

Near binary, because a ramp gantry is a flat $0.75 however little road follows
it. Counting the gantries, for an eastbound run:

- On at Springdale: $0.75 ramp + $1.53 + $0.75 = **$3.03**, more than the whole
  road for less of it.
- Off at Harris Branch: $1.53 + $0.75 ramp = **$2.28**, exactly what going all
  the way costs.
- On at Giles for the eastern half only: $0.75 ramp + $0.75 Parmer = **$1.50**,
  the one partial run that is cheaper than the whole. It gives up the
  signal-dense western half and keeps the Harris Branch and Parmer bypass, so it
  is worth it only when the backup is specifically at Parmer.
- Coming off US 183 rather than along 290 adds $0.75; taking the flyover onto
  SH 130 instead of staying on free 290 adds $0.77.

So there is nothing here to plan a route around. The decision is pay or don't.

## Soft spots

- The 10-to-15-minute peak figure is driver-reported. The direction is solid,
  the size is not.
- The 4 minutes rests on 3 or 4 of the 7 signals costing about 60 seconds each,
  which is an estimate.
- The partial-trip sums assume the Giles entrance ramp puts a car past the Giles
  mainline plaza, which the gantry list implies but does not state.
