# Creatine

What he takes, what dose the evidence supports, and the one thing it interferes with.
Written November 12, 2026, after he asked whether 10 g beats 5 g for mental performance.
His second creatine question; the first, October 29, 2026, was whether to pause it before
a blood draw.

## What he takes

- Creatine monohydrate powder, by the tub scoop. A level scoop is 5 g.
- September 14, 2026: 1 scoop (5 g).
- Paused for the Function Health blood draw of November 11, 2026.
- November 12, 2026: resumed at 10 g.

## The dose

**5 g/day saturates muscle. 10 g/day is the dose the brain research actually uses.**

- Muscle: 3-5 g/day maintains full stores once saturated. A larger athlete may need 5-10 g.
  At 143 lb he is at the low end, so 5 g is plenty for the gym. More does nothing there.
- Brain: creatine crosses the blood-brain barrier slowly, so the brain needs more than the
  muscle does. 2-5 g/day raises brain phosphocreatine about 4-6%; 10 g/day for 8 weeks or
  20 g/day for 7 days raises it about 9-10%. Most cognition trials dose 10-20 g/day.
- So the 10 g he heard about is real, and it is the brain dose rather than a better muscle
  dose.

**Safety is not the question.** Up to 30 g/day for 5 years shows no adverse effect in healthy
people (ISSN position stand, 2017), and 20 g/day for 5 days or 10 g/day for up to 5 years
did not change creatinine clearance, GFR, tubular resorption or glomerular permeability.
10 g/day is well inside that.

**The benefit is the question, and it is weaker than the headlines.**

- A 6-week randomised dose-response trial in healthy young adults at 10 g/day and 20 g/day
  found no improvement in cognitive performance and no change in prefrontal activation.
- A meta-analysis of 16 RCTs found a real effect on memory and processing speed but none on
  overall cognition or executive function.
- The clear wins are under stress or deficit: a single high dose improved cognition and
  processing speed during sleep deprivation, and a 20 g/day, 8-week pilot in Alzheimer's
  raised brain creatine 11% with gains in memory, executive function and reading
  recognition.
- Vegetarians and low-meat eaters respond more, having a lower baseline. He eats meat daily,
  so his baseline is already decent and the headroom is smaller.

**What this adds up to:** 10 g/day is a cheap, safe bet with a modest and uncertain payoff
for someone who already sleeps and eats well, and a better one on short sleep or a hard
work week. 5 g/day loses nothing in the gym.

## Practical notes

- Cost: monohydrate runs about $0.03-0.05 per gram, so 10 g/day is roughly $10-15 a month
  against $5-7 at 5 g. Not a reason either way.
- GI upset at 10 g is the one real side effect, and it comes from taking it all at once on
  an empty stomach. Split 5 g morning and 5 g evening if it bothers him.
- Timing does not matter. Daily consistency does: the muscle and brain pools fill over
  weeks, not hours.
- No loading phase needed. Stores fall slowly over about four weeks, so a pause of a week
  or two needs no reload - just resume the daily dose.
- Drink normally. Creatine pulls water into muscle, which is where the 1-3 lb bump on the
  scale in the first weeks comes from. It is not fat and it is not bloat.

## It interferes with one blood test

Supplemental creatine converts to creatinine, so serum creatinine reads 0.1-0.3 mg/dL high
while supplementing and creatinine-based eGFR reads low - a kidney number that looks
impaired when the kidney is fine. **At 10 g/day the artifact is larger than at 5 g, so the
washout before the next panel should be the full 7 days rather than 5.**

Unaffected: cystatin C and cystatin C eGFR (not made in muscle), lipids, ApoB, Lp(a),
hs-CRP, A1c, fasting insulin, thyroid, liver enzymes, hormones. The Function panel includes
cystatin C, which gives a clean kidney reading either way.
