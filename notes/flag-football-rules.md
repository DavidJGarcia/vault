# Youth flag football: snaps and rushing the QB

Asked Saturday, October 17, 2026 at 4:27 pm: "What are the rules around snaps and
rushing the QB in youth flag football?" Answered from the NFL FLAG rulebook, the USA
Football 7-on-7 flag book, and N Zone Sports Central Texas, the league that runs flag
in the Austin–Manor area.

**Rules vary by league and by division.** Everything below is the common core; the
local rulebook wins, and the two places leagues most often differ are whether the QB
may run and whether illegal rushing is a replay or an automatic first down.

## The snap

- The ball is snapped from the ground to the quarterback in one fluid motion. Between
  the legs is the standard; **side-saddle (shotgun from the hip) is allowed in the
  youngest divisions** — K–2nd, 3rd–4th and the girls' divisions in NFL FLAG, and
  3–4 year olds in N Zone. 5th grade and up must snap between the legs.
- The quarterback is simply whoever receives the snap.
- The center becomes an eligible receiver once the ball is snapped, but the ball may
  **not be handed straight back to the center**.
- A muffed snap is not a fumble: there are no fumbles in flag. The ball is dead where
  it lands. Some leagues let a snap that skips in *front* of the QB be played on, and
  blow one that goes *over* the QB's head dead at the spot.
- No offensive line, no blocking, no motion at the snap beyond one man in the
  backfield in some books.

## Rushing the quarterback

- **Every rusher must be at least 7 yards behind the line of scrimmage at the snap.**
  The rush line is an imaginary line across the field 7 yards back, marked with a cone
  or called by the referee.
- **Any number of defenders may rush.** Everyone else may defend as far forward as the
  line of scrimmage but may not cross it until the ball is handed off.
- A rusher who leaves early may return behind the rush line, reset, and then rush
  legally.
- **Starting inside the 7 and crossing the line is illegal rushing: 5 yards.** NFL
  FLAG books replay the down; several youth books make it 5 yards and an automatic
  first down.
- **A handoff turns the rule off.** Once the ball is handed off, the 7-yard rule ends
  and all defenders may cross the line of scrimmage.
- **The 7-second pass clock.** The QB has 7 seconds to get rid of the ball; past that
  the play is dead, the down is used and it is a 5-yard penalty on the offense. The
  clock also ends on a handoff or when the defense crosses the line.
- **QB runs:** NFL FLAG lets a QB who has been rushed run past the line of scrimmage.
  N Zone Central Texas and many recreation leagues forbid it outright — the QB must
  hand off, pitch or pass. Check the local book before teaching it.
- No-run zones (5 yards before each goal line and midfield) force a pass play, so
  nothing can be run out of them even on a handoff. K–1st and 2nd–3rd divisions often
  have no-run zones removed.
