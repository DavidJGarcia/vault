# Football recruiting stars

Where the high school recruiting rankings come from and what decides a player's star
count. Written October 9, 2026, after two asks in the same minute: "what does it mean to
be a four-star recruit" and "where do the rankings come from and what decides how many
stars somebody gets".

## Who makes them

Three private media companies, none of them the NCAA, a conference or a school:

- **247Sports** — owned by CBS Sports. Grades on a 0–110 scale.
- **Rivals** — the oldest of the three; merged with On3 and now published under the
  Rivals name. Grades on a 100-point scale; older write-ups cite its legacy scale, where
  6.1 was a five-star and 6.0–5.8 a four-star.
- **ESPN** — 0–100 scale, 80 the four-star line.

A fourth number, the **247Sports Composite** (and Rivals' equivalent **Industry
Ranking**), is an algorithm averaging all three. That is what people mean by "industry
consensus", and it exists because the three disagree: a player can be a four-star on one
site and a three-star on another.

## How a player gets evaluated

- **Camps matter more than games.** A regional analyst might make one or two Friday
  nights in a fall, but sees 200 players in a day at a camp — Under Armour, Elite 11,
  the Rivals camp series, combines. 247Sports analyst Jeff Hansen: "they can go and see
  200 kids at a camp."
- **Film.** 247Sports requires an evaluator to watch film before a rating goes out; Hudl
  highlight tape is the common currency.
- **Measurables and projection** — height, weight, speed, frame — projected four or five
  years forward.
- **Offer sheets are an input.** Power 4 offers are considered but do not automatically
  move a rating, which makes the thing partly circular: offers lift rankings and rankings
  draw offers.
- **The projection is to the NFL, not to college.** Both services grade pro potential.
  Rivals: a five-star is "an immediate contributor in college and a potential first round
  NFL draft pick."

## What actually decides the star count

Stars are a cut line on the numeric grade, and the final call belongs to a **national
committee**, not the regional scout who filed the evaluation: nine members at 247Sports,
five at Rivals, where the ratings committee is chaired by the Director of Scouting
(Charles Power, with national scout Cody Bellaire and analysts Greg Biggins and Allen
Trieu).

The top band is a **quota, not a threshold**. 247Sports sets exactly 32 five-stars per
class to mirror the 32 first-round NFL picks; four-stars run roughly 330–350, about the
top 10 percent of ranked recruits. So the rating is relative: a player rises by
displacing someone, and rankings move all cycle as camps and senior film come in.

## The honest caveats

- **Coverage is thin.** Over a million boys play high school football a year; Rivals
  star-rates about 6,600 prospects. Most players are never evaluated at all.
- **Access costs money.** The camp circuit — travel, fees, trainers — favours families
  who can pay, and a late developer who skips it can be missed entirely. Zach Wilson was
  a three-star and went second overall in 2021.
- **Rivals now sells evaluations.** Since September 24, 2026 a high school athlete can
  pay $499 (introductory $399) for a Rivals scouting report, a grade and "ranking
  consideration". Rivals' line is that ratings "are earned, never bought" — what the
  money buys is being looked at.
