# What Index is not being used for

Asked Saturday November 7, 2026: "What should I be using you for that I'm not?"
Answered from the record counts and the vault as they stood that morning, not from
a list of features.

## What is already well used

Food (548 meal rows since September 15), the gym (203 exercise sets, and the `gym`
view rebuilt weekly), nutrition questions (42), weigh-ins (33), questions answered
from the world (187), shopping and carts (98), the to-do list (42), reminders (7
live timers), the Christmas gift list (10 ideas, actively priced and researched),
and the YNAB memo work every weekly pass. None of that is a gap.

## The four gaps

### 1. Work - the biggest by far

- `work_clock`: 2 rows, both September 28, 2026. Nothing since.
- `review_note`: 3 rows, all from one evening, October 27. All three are the
  **upward** half. The self-review half of the `review` view - accomplishments and
  impact, skills and growth, challenges, goals - is still empty eleven days later,
  and that page exists because he asked for it.
- So: his body is logged in detail every day and his job is not logged at all.

What to do instead: one line when something goes well or badly at work, said the
day it happens. "Self review: shipped the FDE pilot, client signed off." Specifics
spoken in November are what a December review is written out of; a competency
phrase remembered in December is not. The same line is also a searchable record of
what he actually did this year, which nothing else here holds.

Still unbuilt, and related: `notes/work-calendar-access.md` (October 21) worked out
three ways to read his work Outlook calendar read-only. Nothing here reads a Graph
calendar yet, so the briefing cannot say what his day holds. That is a build, not a
setting.

### 2. People, especially the kids

- `person_fact`: 14 rows, last on October 20.
- `catch_up`: 2 rows, both September 20, though `people/catch-up.md` exists.
- Ten people files for a wife, six children, his dad and others.

The small facts are the ones that do not survive on their own: what a kid is into
this month, a thing one of them said, a milestone, what the teacher mentioned, how
his dad sounded on the phone. Said once, they come back later - in a gift idea, in
a catch-up, in the briefing. Not said, they are gone.

### 3. "When did I last" - household upkeep

- `litter_emptying`: 6 rows (and the only thing with a standing rule behind it).
- `nail_trim`: 2 rows.
- Nothing else. No air filter, no fridge filter, no oil change, no AC service, no
  water softener, no smoke-detector battery, no lawn treatment, no pest control.

"When did I last change the air filter" is one of the strongest things this system
does and it is fed almost nothing. It costs four words at the time - "changed the
air filter" - and the answer is then permanent, with the gap between changes
computable.

### 4. Money, before the purchase rather than after

The tracking is retrospective: his charges are categorized and memo'd after they
land, and the weekly pass reconciles them. Almost nothing is asked beforehand -
"what is left in groceries", "can I afford this", "what did I spend on eating out
last month". The budget is already connected; the question is the cheap part.

## Smaller things, if any of them appeal

- Health beyond food and the gym: sleep, medication, a symptom, what a doctor said.
  Nothing of this is on record anywhere.
- A view for anything recurring he reads more than once. `gym` and `review` prove
  the pattern; house projects, a reading list, the kids' school year could each be
  one.
- Decisions with their reasoning, so a choice made in March can be re-read in
  September with what was known at the time.
