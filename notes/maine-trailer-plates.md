# Why semi trailers carry Maine plates

Asked three times in four minutes on October 17, 2026, driving: why the Maine
plates, is Maine really cheaper, and then why anyone registers a trailer
anywhere else.

## Why Maine works

- Trailers are not apportionable under the International Registration Plan.
  Only the power unit is plated in the carrier's base state; IRP section 515
  gives a trailer properly registered in any jurisdiction full reciprocity
  everywhere. So the trailer can be plated wherever is cheapest, and the
  tractor cannot.
- Maine charges $20 a year for a semitrailer over 2,000 lb (29-A section 511),
  $10.50 under; multi-year terms renew at the same rate, and the services sell
  five years for about $99.
- No Maine residency or address. No municipal excise tax and no sales tax for a
  non-resident trailer in interstate commerce. Up to 100 trailers filed at once,
  by mail or online, without appearing.
- Roughly 800,000 semi trailers carry Maine plates against about 27,000 tied to
  a Maine address (2016 reporting).

## Why it is not uniquely cheap

- Texas token trailer plate: $15 a year, cheaper on paper, but only for a
  trailer pulled by a carrier already based and apportioned in Texas, and the
  purchase carries 6.25% state sales tax.
- Illinois: $270 to $1,602 a year by weight. California: $28 plus a weight fee
  up to $1,016 on a commercial trailer.

Maine is not the cheapest number. It is the one that is for sale to anyone, in
bulk, untaxed.

## Why anyone registers elsewhere

1. **It only covers interstate commerce.** A trailer that works inside one
   state has to be plated in that state. Massachusetts requires that a trailer
   pulled by a Massachusetts-registered vehicle be registered there too, and
   fines about $500 for a Maine plate on a local trailer; other states take the
   same line that a trailer which lives in-state is not properly registered
   elsewhere.
2. **The home state's sales or use tax is still owed** where the trailer is
   domiciled. A Maine plate does not erase it, and that is what gets audited.
3. **A private trailer follows its owner.** A utility or boat trailer is
   registered where the owner lives.
4. **Permanent plates often beat $20 a year.** Arizona $125 once, Alaska $55
   for a non-commercial trailer, Virginia a one-time fee for private, for-hire
   and rental trailers, California a $20 permanent trailer ID for
   non-commercial. One fee, never renewed.
5. **It is a fleet play.** The paperwork runs through a third-party service with
   its own annual fee, so the saving only pays on many trailers.
