# Meat temperatures

Asked twice at the grill on October 11, 2026 — first what internal temp hamburgers
go to, then what medium-well is. Degrees Fahrenheit, measured with an instant-read
probe into the side of the patty to the centre, never down through the top. Colour is
not a sign of doneness: ground beef can brown at 131F and can stay pink past 160F.

## Doneness, beef

| Doneness | Internal temp |
|---|---|
| Rare | 120–125 |
| Medium rare | 130–135 |
| Medium | 140–145 |
| Medium well | 150–155 |
| Well done | 160+ |

## Safety, and why the two numbers differ

- **Whole cuts** (steak, roast) carry bacteria only on the surface, which the sear
  kills. USDA safe minimum: **145F plus a 3-minute rest**. Medium rare is below that
  and is the ordinary risk people accept with a steak.
- **Ground beef** has the surface mixed all through it, so the centre has to get there
  too. USDA safe minimum: **160F**, instantaneous.
- **Poultry, or a patty with ground turkey or chicken in it**: 165F.

## Medium-well burgers without giving up safety

160F is a floor only because it is instant. Lethality is time *and* temperature, and
FSIS's cooking guideline (Appendix A, revised 2021) gives the equivalents for beef:

| Internal temp | Hold for 7-log Salmonella reduction |
|---|---|
| 145 | ~3 minutes |
| 150 | ~72 seconds |
| 155 | ~9–17 seconds |
| 160 | instant |

So **pull the patty at 150F and let it rest a minute under a tent or a closed lid**.
Carryover brings it to about 155 and the hold does the same kill 160F does on contact
— a genuinely medium-well burger, as safe as a well-done one. Below 150 the hold gets
long enough that the patty is cooling faster than it is pasteurising; that is where the
trade stops working.

## Fish: trout and salmon

Asked October 18, 2026 — "do you just cook trout the same way as salmon?" Yes on
method, no on timing.

| Fish | Pull at | Fillet thickness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salmon | 125 | 1–1.5 in | fattest fish most people cook, so it forgives a minute |
| Trout | 130 | under 1 in, often 1/2 in | lean, little collagen, dries in a minute |

- **Same methods**: pan-sear, roast, grill, broil, skin down first, seasoned simply.
  Any salmon recipe works for trout.
- **Half the time.** Pan-sear a trout fillet about 3 minutes skin down and 1 minute
  after the flip; oven 400F for 10–12 minutes. The same salmon fillet takes 4–5
  minutes a side, 12–15 in the oven.
- **Trout skin tears on grill grates** where salmon skin holds. Foil, a cedar plank,
  or cook the trout whole.
- FDA's safe minimum for fish is 145F, which is well past dry for either one; both
  pull temps above are the ordinary risk people accept with good fish, and carryover
  adds a few degrees off the heat.

### His baked salmon, carried over to trout

How he bakes salmon, in his words a minute later the same day: **yellow mustard,
olive oil, salt, pepper, lemon juice.** It carries over, and it suits trout better
than it suits salmon — the oil supplies the fat trout lacks, and the mustard binds
the coat to the fillet instead of letting it run into the pan. Yellow mustard bakes
mild; it does not read as mustard in the finished fish.

Two things change for trout:

- **Lemon juice goes on after it comes out of the oven**, not into the coat before
  baking. Acid sitting on a half-inch lean fillet begins to cure it and leaves the
  surface mushy; a thick fatty salmon fillet shrugs the same marinade off.
- **400F for 10–12 minutes, pull at about 130F**, against the 12–15 minutes a salmon
  fillet of the same recipe takes.
