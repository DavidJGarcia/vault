# Roasted pumpkin seeds

Asked November 4, 2026: "How do I make roasted pumpkin seeds?" — a few days after
Halloween, so read as the seeds out of a carving pumpkin.

## Method

1. **Scoop and rinse.** Pull the seeds out of the pulp and rinse in a colander
   until no strings are left.
2. **Boil 10 minutes in salted water.** About 1/2 teaspoon salt per cup of water.
   This is the step most people skip and the one that matters: the salt gets
   inside the hull, and the boil frees the last of the membrane. Seeds come out
   crisp rather than chewy.
3. **Dry well.** Drain, then pat between towels. Wet seeds steam instead of
   roasting.
4. **Toss with oil and salt.** A tablespoon of olive oil per cup of seeds, plus
   salt. Anything else goes on here too — garlic powder, smoked paprika, chili
   powder, cinnamon and sugar for a sweet batch.
5. **Roast at 325°F for 20–25 minutes**, single layer on a parchment-lined sheet,
   stirring once at the halfway mark. Done when golden brown and they pop or
   crack between the teeth. They crisp further as they cool, so pull them a shade
   earlier than looks right.

## Notes

- Hotter and faster (400°F, 10–15 minutes) works but browns the outside before
  the middle dries; 325°F is the forgiving setting.
- Skip the boil if in a hurry: dry the seeds well, oil, salt, and roast the same
  way. They will be chewier.
- Seeds from a carving pumpkin are bigger and tougher-hulled than the green
  pepitas sold in bags; those are hulled seeds from a different variety and need
  far less time.
- Keep in an airtight jar at room temperature; good for about a week, and they
  go soft before they go bad. A few minutes back in a warm oven re-crisps them.
- Rough numbers: a cup of in-hull roasted seeds is about 285 kcal and 12 g of
  protein, before the oil. Salted seeds carry real sodium, so the tally matters
  if a day is already high.
